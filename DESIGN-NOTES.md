A blue photography album rests clear of the bowl; its transparent click target and cover label follow the illustrated perspective. The TV and record player share one walnut console. The rug and plaid throw follow Sofía’s R6, R9, R3 and T3 selections.

Sofía identifies as Mexican. That is the starting point. Her fluency in English, upbringing between Miami and Mexico, and work in the United States do not authorize describing her as Mexican-American or assigning her a diasporic identity.

## What the research changes

These identities and histories are related, but they are not interchangeable. The Smithsonian’s [¡Printing the Revolution!](https://americanart.si.edu/exhibitions/chicano-graphics) situates Chicano graphics in the U.S. civil-rights and other social movements of the 1960s onward. It also describes varied artistic practices within that history. Chicano is not a label to automatically apply to every Mexican-American person, much less to every Mexican person.

There is no single Mexican artistic style to substitute for that assumption. INBAL’s discussion of [Rufino Tamayo](https://inba.gob.mx/prensa/20787/la-sintesis-de-rufino-tamayo-genero-una-pintura-diferente-en-mexico) describes a particular synthesis of modern painting, Mesoamerican references and popular art, avoiding folkloric reduction. MUAC’s presentation of [Colectivo Cherani](https://muac.unam.mx/exposicion/colectivo-cherani) foregrounds specific Purépecha perspectives, personal work, contemporary practice and the questioning of official stereotypes. These are distinct examples, not a recipe for Sofía’s identity.

My design conclusion: nationality does not supply a set of mandatory objects, colors, musical cues or language mixtures. The room should follow Sofía’s actual selections and personal associations. Her recent saved references for tin hearts, mirrors, textiles, colored glass and domestic interiors remain valid because she chose them. Removing those objects simply because they might also appear in a stereotype would repeat the mistake of overriding her taste.

- Selecting a film brings the camera closer and plays it on the TV itself. Project information stays in a transparent side panel, or a lower panel on phones. The poster and mirror remain physical entry points in the room.

## Music close-up and the first editable pixel object

Sofía requested the same place from above, retaining the TV edge for continuity. The approved wide master remains untouched. The overhead artwork was made with the built-in image tool using that master as a reference (`assets/vinyl-closeup-prompt.txt`). A localized edit removed the added record sleeves (`assets/vinyl-closeup-clean-prompt.txt`). Music selection uses an explicitly approved translucent panel rather than introducing extra physical objects.

The latest direction is to rebuild the whole room as editable pixel art over time, beginning with this player. The close-up now uses seven transparent PNG layers traced onto a 640 × 400 grid. The record, fixed reflections, spindle and arm are independently rendered. Hidden surfaces have been reconstructed so the arm can move. The rest of the room has not yet been converted. This is an editable reconstruction of the existing artwork, not a claim of a new hand-drawn illustration.

SoundCloud entries are correctly described as covers, live recordings and an orchestral recomposition.
